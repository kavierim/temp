What is Motion Magnification?

Motion Magnification is a powerful computer vision technique that allows us to see tiny, often invisible movements in video. By analyzing sub-pixel changes over time, we can amplify specific frequencies of movement, revealing things like a person's pulse, the subtle sway of a building, or the vibrations of an engine.

How This Tool Works

This application implements a real-time version of Eulerian Video Magnification. Unlike traditional "feature tracking" which follows specific objects, Eulerian methods look at pixels as a fixed grid. By comparing current frames with a temporal average (smoothing), we isolate the "motion signal" and multiply it (gain) before rendering it back onto the screen. This updated version uses WebGL 2.0 to perform these calculations on your Graphics Processing Unit (GPU), ensuring high performance even at HD resolutions.

Scientific Use Cases

  • Biometrics: Detecting heart rates through skin color changes and micro-movements.
  • Structural Engineering: Identifying resonant frequencies in bridges or cranes.
  • Mechanical Maintenance: Troubleshooting imbalanced rotors or vibrating machinery.

Key Features

  • GPU Acceleration: Uses WebGL 2.0 for lag-free processing.
  • Spatial Decomposition: High-quality spatial filtering to reduce edge halos.
  • Live Data Plotting: Graph motion intensity in real-time.
  • Frequency Filtering: Isolate specific vibration speeds.
